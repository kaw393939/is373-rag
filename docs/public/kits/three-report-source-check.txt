ONE INCIDENT, THREE REPORTS
An Ordo Knowledge Lab exercise by Keith Williams
Time: about ten minutes. Use paper, a text editor, or a partner.
All names, reports, and quotations below are fictional.

THE QUESTION
How many independent accounts in this file describe a moving light?
Cite the exact line that supports your answer. Say what remains uncertain.

SOURCE A • Harbor log • 9:15 p.m.
A1. Alex Lee, a dock worker, wrote this entry after an evening shift.
A2. “I saw a white light move from the crane toward the east pier.”
A3. The entry gives no distance, speed, or explanation.

SOURCE B • Neighborhood bulletin • the next morning
B1. The bulletin names Alex Lee’s harbor log as its source.
B2. It quotes the log: “I saw a white light move from the crane toward the east pier.”
B3. The bulletin contains no additional eyewitness interview.

SOURCE C • Dispatch note • 9:17 p.m.
C1. A second observer, identified only as R., reported seeing a light near the east pier.
C2. “It stayed in the same place for as long as I could see it.”
C3. The note does not establish whether R. and Alex saw the same object.

READ, DECIDE, CHECK
1. Mark which sources describe motion and who observed it in each.
2. Answer in two sentences. Use line IDs so another person can repeat your check.
3. Ask a partner to find one claim your answer makes that the records cannot settle.
4. Change one detail: suppose B had interviewed a different eyewitness. What would
   you need to see in B before changing your count?

FOR THE DISCUSSION AFTERWARD
A2 supports one independent account of motion. B1–B3 show that B repeats A.
C2 supports a separate observation of a stationary light. C3 leaves the identity
of the light open. “One moving object and one stationary object” exceeds the evidence.
Another defensible answer can use different words if the same links can be checked.

If you use an AI assistant, ask it the original question. Open each line it cites.
Which distinction survived in its answer? Which one did you have to put back?
